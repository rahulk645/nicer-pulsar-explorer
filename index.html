Dense Matter Equation of State (EOS) Synthesis

How superseding NICER mass-radius measurements challenge nuclear physics models and inform exotic state transitions.

Nuclear Tension

PREX-II / CREX vs. NICER Softening

Terrestrial neutron skin experiments (PREX-II on $^{208}\text{Pb}$) favor a large nuclear symmetry energy slope ($L \gtrsim 70 \text{ MeV}$), predicting stiff EOS and canonical radii $R_{1.4} > 13.5 \text{ km}$. Conversely, superseding NICER data (J0030 downward shift to $\sim 11.7 \text{ km}$, J0614 at $\sim 10.3 \text{ km}$, and J1614 at $\sim 10.1 \text{ km}$) require much softer matter at intermediate densities.

Theoretical Resolution Mechanism:

Theoretical models resolve this conflict within Relativistic Mean-Field (RMF) frameworks by introducing sudden symmetry energy softening at $\approx 2\rho_{\text{sat}}$, mediated by isoscalar-isovector meson mixing.

Exotic Physics

Phase Transitions & Quark Stars

Simultaneously sustaining massive objects like PSR J0740+6620 ($2.07 M_\odot, R \approx 12.5 \text{ km}$) while accommodating extreme compactness in PSR J0614−3329 and J1614−2230 ($R \approx 10 \text{ km}$) disfavors simple monotonic nucleonic equations of state.

Key Astrophysical Implication:

Strong evidence for first-order phase transitions to deconfined quark matter, hyperons, or pure Strange Quark Stars (SQS) (Bodmer-Witten hypothesis), followed by rapid speed-of-sound ($c_s^2$) stiffening at core densities.
